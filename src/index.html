YouTube setup and advanced API options

YouTube needs a Google Desktop OAuth app once. Twitch and Kick do not need developer credentials in Official chat mode. Twitch API mode below is optional.

Register this exact OAuth redirect: http://localhost:47823/callback
1. Create a Google Cloud project and enable YouTube Data API v3. 2. In Google Auth Platform, configure the consent screen and add your Google account under Audience → Test users. 3. Create a client of type Desktop app. 4. Paste its client ID and client secret above and save. 5. Open Connections → Sign in to Google and select your Ty0x7 YouTube / Brand Account. No video URL is needed.

YouTube requests the platform’s chat read/write scope, which Google describes broadly. Chat uses streaming, with polling only if streaming is unsupported. API usage consumes your project’s quota. Tokens stay encrypted with Windows; chat messages stay in memory.
A QUICK TOUR

ChatHub controls

  1. Make the connection.

    Right-click anywhere and choose Connections, or press Ctrl+Shift+C. Enter your Twitch or Kick channel username, or your YouTube channel URL (youtube.com/@Ty0x7).

  2. Sign in on your terms.

    For Twitch and Kick, choose Sign in in Connections and use their official login pages. For YouTube, add your Google Desktop app credentials in Settings, then authorize your channel through your browser. Google sign-in on Kick opens externally and may not establish a Kick session in ChatHub; use Kick’s direct account login.

  3. Settle into your space.

    One chat fills the workspace. Two split it evenly. Three get their own columns. Hide a platform from the right-click menu, and move columns with the arrows in Connections. Drag a platform’s slim top label to move the window.

  4. Keep your community safe.

    Right-click a Twitch or YouTube message to delete it or apply a 10-minute timeout. Live actions require confirmation and moderator permissions. Twitch also has slow mode controls. Kick retains its own native chat and moderation controls.

ChatHub has one local workspace. Preview messages are fictional, never sent to a platform. Native dashboards opened in your system browser have a separate browser login. Provider login challenges, account permissions, live chat availability, and quotas still apply.
